Lake Salda: Türkiye's twin of Jezero Crater, examined side by side

Jezero Crater held a lake about 3.5 billion years ago, and Perseverance is searching its delta and carbonate rocks for signs of ancient life. NASA pairs it with one Earth lake: Lake Salda in Burdur, Türkiye, whose white shore is made of hydromagnesite, a carbonate similar to those found at Jezero, built partly by microbial mounds called microbialites. Our search scored 70 places against Jezero, and Salda ranks 1. Part of its evidence comes from NASA's own comparison, so that rank confirms the method more than it discovers anything; this page shows what makes Salda a twin, where it is not one, and what could be tested there.

      Salda matches Jezero's ancient lake, not today's Mars: it has liquid water, Earth's air pressure and a mild climate. It is a place to rehearse how carbonate rocks and microbial structures are sampled and read, before the same work is done on Martian samples.
