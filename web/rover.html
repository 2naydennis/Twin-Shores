What Jezero may have looked like about 3.5 billion years ago, when it held a lake. Nobody knows what it really looked like.

What is evidence and what is imagined

Evidence: a lake. Jezero held a lake fed by a river; Perseverance found lake sediments and a delta (NASA).

Evidence: the basin. The ground is NASA MOLA elevation data. The water is filled to , just below the lowest gap in the crater rim in that data (on the south-east side), covering about . The lake often stood lower than its outlet (study of the outlet valley), so one fixed level is a simplification.

Evidence: carbonates. Orbiters detect carbonate minerals along the crater's inner margin (our data). The pale band at the waterline stands for them.

Imagined: life. No life has been found on Mars. The rounded mounds are invented microbial structures, modelled on the microbialites that grow on Lake Salda's carbonate shore, and drawn far larger than life.

Imagined: the look. The sky, the colour of the water and ground, and the exact shoreline are guesses.

Lake Salda has no outlet, so its level depends on rain, groundwater and evaporation. Satellite studies and local reports say it has been falling. You choose how far it falls here; nobody has published how far it will.

What is reported, and what this view assumes

Reported: the level is falling. A study of Landsat images from 2004, 2014 and 2024 found a steady decrease (2025 study), and a study of the shoreline from 1975 to 2019 found it had moved by up to 556 m in places (Dereli and Tercan, 2020).

Reported: so far the loss is small. From 1984 to 2018 the lake's water area stayed above 90% of its largest extent (METU thesis). The lake is deep and steep-sided, so a falling level shows less than on a shallow lake.

Reported by local officials, in the news: the shore drew back about 25 to 30 m in ten years (CNN Türk, 2020), and about 20 m with more than half a metre of level in one dry summer (Habertürk). These are statements, not measurements we checked.

Causes named in those reports: drought and low rainfall, falling groundwater and boreholes, irrigation ponds and dams on the streams that feed the lake, water-hungry crops, and tourism pressure (Sözcü).

Why it matters for Mars research. Salda's microbialites grow in the shallow water along the shore (Balci and others, 2020). A falling level strands that zone first. The pale mounds here stand for stranded microbialites; where they are and how many is invented, and they are drawn far larger than life.

Assumed: the lake bed. NASA's elevation data records only the lake surface. The bed here is a guess: depth grows evenly from the shore to the published maximum of 196 m at the point farthest from land. The area and shoreline figures follow from that guess, so they are rough.

Not shown: changes to water chemistry, the surrounding land, rainfall or temperature.
