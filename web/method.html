Method and results

Which lakes on Earth most resemble the lake that once filled Jezero Crater?

Mission teams rehearse on Earth before they fly. NASA pairs Jezero Crater, where Perseverance is looking for signs of ancient life, with one Earth lake: Lake Salda in Türkiye. We compared 70 places with Jezero on eight variables to see whether open data and published studies point to more.

01Result

Lake Salda, the analog NASA already lists, comes out first. The lakes directly behind it are closed-basin, alkaline lakes that NASA does not list: Lake Van, Lake Erçek and Lake Burdur. Lake Van is already studied in a Mars context by researchers, so we call it a candidate, not a discovery. Lake Erçek, its little-studied neighbour, rests on a single conference abstract and needs field confirmation.

02What is compared

similarity = 1 − |place − Jezero| ÷ spread of that variable across all places
score = weighted average of the similarities available for that place

A variable with no value on either side is left out of that place's score. It is never counted as zero. All weights are currently 1.

03Does the ranking hold?

Try your own weights. Move a slider to change how much a variable counts. The top ten updates at once. Set a weight to 0 to leave that variable out.

Random weights. The eight weights were redrawn at random 5,000 times and the ranking recomputed each time.

Leave one variable out. The ranking was recomputed eight times, each time without one variable.

Second elevation model. Terrain slope was measured again with the Copernicus EU-DEM and compared with the NASA/METI ASTER values used in the scores.

Known analogs. On temperature, pressure and slope alone, known Mars analogs such as the Atacama Desert and Death Valley rank low. Climate and slope do not capture what makes a place resemble Jezero; water chemistry, minerals and sediments do.

04Limits

  • No place on Earth comes near Mars's air pressure, cold or radiation. Every site here has more than 100 times Jezero's pressure.
  • Jezero's ancient lake has no measured pH. That variable compares with the conditions inferred from its carbonates.
  • The evidence from published studies was gathered with an AI tool. Each entry on the map shows whether its source was read directly or taken from a summary. Entries not yet confirmed by the team are marked.
  • Only 40 of the 62 lakes have published evidence entered. Lakes without entries may match better than their score suggests.
  • The list of places is a choice. The search first covered all 148 natural lakes in Türkiye that Wikidata lists. On 2026-11-14 the team removed 111 of them that had no published evidence and lay more than 150 km from Lake Salda, and added 24 lakes from other countries: carbonate and microbialite lakes, impact-crater lakes, soda lakes, and five well-known large lakes expected to be poor analogs. Because each measured variable is scaled by its spread across all places, changing the list shifts every score a little.
  • The robustness tests check the arithmetic, not the evidence. A wrong entry carries through all of them.
  • The project covers Mars only. The challenge also names Moon base locations; those need different variables and were not attempted.

05Data and tools

  • NASA: POWER climatology (temperature, pressure, evaporation); ASTER GDEM with Japan's METI (Earth slope); Mars Global Surveyor MOLA (Jezero slope and elevation); SRTM (terrain view); NSSDCA Mars Fact Sheet; Planetary Analog Explorer (known analogs).
  • European Union and ESA, Copernicus: EU-DEM (slope cross-check); Sentinel-2 cloudless imagery by EOX (map background).
  • Other: Wikidata (list of Türkiye's natural lakes; coordinates of the lakes added from other countries); OpenStreetMap; published studies linked from each site.
  • AI use: the data scripts, this website's code and first drafts of the text were written with Claude (Anthropic). ChatGPT was used for background reading. Every use is listed in the repository. No AI-generated images are used.
