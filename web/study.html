What Lake Salda can teach us about Jezero Crater, and what it cannot

Three parts: a scientific report by discipline, a comparative map of the two basins made with one method on both worlds, and our own assessment of which features of Salda are worth studying for Mars. Every statement carries a label saying how it is known.

00How each statement is known

    A measurement on a lake shore and a detection from orbit are not equally certain, and Salda is a lake today while Jezero's lake is reconstructed from rock about 3.5 billion years old.     02Comparative map

    Both maps were made the same way from NASA elevation data: find the lake, find the land that drains into it, find where the largest streams enter, and find where the water would leave if it rose. The letters pair a zone at Salda with its counterpart at Jezero.

    Computed stream inlets

    Positions and areas are computed from the elevation grids, not surveyed. They count only land inside each map, so Jezero's figures are far smaller than its real river catchments.

        04How this page was made

        The basin analysis is a calculation on elevation grids, repeatable with one command. It is a model: the two grids differ in resolution, catchments are cut off at the map edge, and nothing was checked on the ground. The report text was drafted by an AI tool from the sources listed beside each statement, and needs the team's review.
